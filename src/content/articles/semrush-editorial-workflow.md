A research tool can make topic discovery faster. It cannot decide whether a story is worth publishing, whether a source is credible, or whether a draft sounds like your publication. Good editorial workflows make those responsibilities explicit.

The workflow below uses Semrush as a research and review aid. It is not a claim that software can replace a writer, editor, subject-matter expert, or fact-checker.

## Stage 1: Write the assignment before opening a tool

Record the intended reader, the question the piece answers, the format, the evidence needed, and the action you hope the reader can take. Add the publication's voice, accessibility requirements, and any topics that require expert or legal review.

This short assignment keeps keyword suggestions from turning into an unbounded list of articles.

## Stage 2: Explore questions and related topics

Use a seed phrase in Keyword Magic Tool or Topic Research to find vocabulary and adjacent questions. Save a focused shortlist. Then inspect the live search results and reliable sources to understand intent and identify what existing pages miss.

Treat keyword volume and difficulty as estimates for prioritization. They are not a forecast of traffic, a measure of importance to your readers, or a reason to cover a topic outside your expertise.

## Stage 3: Build a brief with an editor in charge

A useful brief should tell a writer what the reader needs—not prescribe an article by copying competitor headings. Include:

- The page's purpose and intended audience
- The primary question and genuinely related subquestions
- Primary sources, expert interviews, or data the article needs
- What the piece will add that a generic summary does not
- A reviewer, owner, and date for checking time-sensitive claims

An editor should remove suggestions that do not serve the assignment.

## Stage 4: Draft and edit for the reader

Write from sources and expertise. Use plain language, explain limitations, link to relevant primary material, and make the next step easy to understand. Keep house style, accessibility, and accuracy standards in the assignment instead of relying on an automated score to enforce them.

## Stage 5: Use optimization checks as questions, not commands

If your team uses SEO Writing Assistant or another content checker, review its feedback after the substance and structure are in place. Ask whether a suggestion improves clarity or coverage. Skip anything that creates awkward repetition, unsupported claims, or an unnatural voice.

## Stage 6: Publish, learn, and update

Record the publication date, reviewers, and sources. After the page has had time to collect data, look at first-party performance, reader questions, and the accuracy of the information. Update the content when evidence or the audience's needs change—not simply to raise a score.

## The Fatorati takeaway

Semrush can support parts of a repeatable editorial process. The publication still owns the research standard, the voice, and the final decision to publish.
